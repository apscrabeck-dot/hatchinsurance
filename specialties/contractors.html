— Requirements —

What Arizona Law Requires

01

ROC License Bond (ARS § 32-1152)

Every licensed contractor must post a surety bond as a condition of licensure. Residential general bonds commonly run $5,000–$15,000.

02

Workers' Compensation

Required for any contractor with 1+ employees.

03

General Liability Insurance

Not mandated by the ROC for licensure, but nearly every commercial GC and property manager requires proof, typically $1M/$2M, before work begins.

— Coverage —

What We Recommend Beyond the Minimums

General Liability

$1M/$2M is the de facto standard requested on nearly every commercial job's certificate of insurance.

Builder's Risk

Covers a structure under construction: needed project-by-project or annually for GCs running multiple jobs.

Tools & Equipment (Inland Marine)

Covers tools and equipment away from the shop or in transit, not covered by commercial property.

Commercial Auto + Hired/Non-Owned

Covers company trucks and employees driving personal vehicles for work errands.

Umbrella / Excess Liability

Commercial job sites carry real injury exposure; GCs increasingly require $2M+ in umbrella limits from subs.

Professional Liability / E&O

Relevant for design-build work or specialty trades where a system failure could trigger a claim beyond property damage.

— Nuance —

What Makes Contractor Insurance Different

The GL audit

Most policies are audited annually on payroll and subcontractor cost; underreporting sub spend is the most common surprise.

COI turnaround

Commercial GCs often need a same-day certificate before a sub can start work; slow turnaround costs contractors jobs.

1099 subcontractor risk

Using subs without their own GL and workers' comp can push their payroll onto your policy at audit time.

Trade-specific carriers

Electrical and HVAC contractors face different underwriting than general residential remodelers.

— FAQ —

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need general liability insurance to get my ROC license?

Not directly: the ROC requires a surety bond, not GL. But in practice, nearly every commercial GC will require proof of GL before you're on a job site.

What's the ROC bond, and is that the same thing as insurance?

No, the bond protects consumers and the state; GL protects you and third parties from claims. You need both.

How much does contractor insurance cost in Arizona?

It depends on trade classification, payroll, revenue, and claims history. Give us a call for an accurate quote.

I use 1099 subs. Am I covered for their work?

Only if structured properly. Require every sub to carry their own coverage and provide a certificate naming you as additional insured.

Do I need builder's risk if I'm only doing tenant improvement work?

Usually, yes: it covers materials and work-in-progress not yet covered by a standard property policy.
